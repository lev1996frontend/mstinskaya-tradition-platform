# User Flows

## 1. Visitor Flow

An unauthenticated visitor can explore the public side of the platform without creating an account. The visitor may review information about Mstinskaya Tradition, read public rules and regulations, browse upcoming or past tournaments, view athlete and club profiles, browse the public equipment catalog, and explore available courses.

This public experience is intended to inform the community and help visitors understand the content and structure of the platform without requiring sign-in.

---

## 2. Registration and Account Flow

A new user begins by registering an account. After registration, the user creates a profile and fills out the information needed for their role and public presence. If the platform requires email verification, the user confirms their email address before continuing.

The user may then choose interests and areas of activity. These interests help surface relevant content, but they do not define the user's official role on the platform. A user account is not equal to a role.

Later, a user may become one of several roles, including athlete, instructor, judge, or organizer. The same person may hold multiple roles over time or simultaneously. The role assignment happens after account creation and is not simply a single fixed account setting.

---

## 3. Athlete Flow

A person wants to participate in the community as an athlete.

1. Registration.
   - The user creates an account and completes a profile.
2. Creating athlete profile.
   - The user adds personal and athletic information that is relevant to their participation.
3. Joining a club.
   - The athlete may join a club and become part of an organization or training group.
4. Studying training materials.
   - The athlete learns from courses, methodology, and educational resources.
5. Registering for tournaments.
   - The athlete identifies the event, category, and any participation requirements.
6. Participating in competitions.
   - The athlete takes part in the tournament and follows the schedule and rules.
7. Viewing results and history.
   - The athlete reviews their matches, outcomes, and event history.
8. Viewing rating.
   - The athlete can review current standing or rating history as defined by the official rules.

This flow connects personal identity, club membership, education, competition participation, and performance history.

---

## 4. Instructor Flow

An instructor wants access to educational materials and training content.

1. Registration.
   - The user registers and creates a profile.
2. Role confirmation.
   - The user is assigned or confirmed in the instructor role.
3. Access to instructor materials.
   - The user gains access to instructional materials relevant to teaching and methodology.
4. Studying methodology.
   - The instructor reviews structured educational content and training guidance.
5. Completing courses.
   - The instructor completes educational modules and assessments as required.
6. Receiving certificates.
   - The instructor may receive certification or completion recognition.
7. Managing students in the future.
   - The platform may later allow lesson or student management, but that is a future capability.

The instructor flow is focused on educational access, methodological learning, and professional development.

---

## 5. Judge Flow

A person wants to become a judge.

1. Access judging materials.
   - The user explores educational content about judging practices and official rules.
2. Study rules.
   - The user reviews rule sets and judging documentation related to the Mstinskaya Tradition.
3. Watch example situations.
   - The user reviews examples, disputed situations, and practical judging scenarios.
4. Complete tests.
   - The user completes required educational assessments or evaluations.
5. Receive judge status.
   - The user is recognized or assigned the appropriate judging qualification or status.
6. Participate in tournaments.
   - The judge joins judging panels for live or recorded events.
7. Preserve judging history.
   - The platform records the judge's participation and decision history without replacing the real-world judging process.

This flow emphasizes judge education and record keeping. It does not replace real-world judging or simulate live online scoring.

---

## 6. Organizer Flow

An organizer creates and manages a tournament.

1. Create tournament.
   - The organizer defines the event and overall tournament context.
2. Select regulation version.
   - The organizer chooses the exact ruleset and version that apply to the event.
3. Create categories.
   - The organizer sets the tournament categories and divisions.
4. Add participants.
   - The organizer registers athletes or participant entries for each category.
5. Create schedule.
   - The organizer establishes the event timeline and fight schedule.
6. Assign judges.
   - The organizer assigns the judging panel for each fight or stage.
7. Record results.
   - The organizer records official fight results and final event outcomes.
8. Publish results.
   - The organizer makes the results visible to participants and the public where appropriate.
9. Add photos/videos/documents.
   - The organizer may attach event coverage, documentation, and media records.

The organizer flow is driven by event management, regulatory compliance, and historical recordkeeping.

---

## 7. Tournament Participant Flow

Before tournament:

- registration;
- category selection;
- confirmation.

During tournament:

- schedule review;
- fight participation;
- result confirmation.

After tournament:

- history review;
- rating update if applicable;
- media access and documentation.

A participant may be a registered athlete or a person taking part in a specific event. The platform distinguishes between the athlete identity and the tournament-specific participation record, so historical participation remains accurate.

---

## 8. Education Flow

A user moves through learning content in a structured way:

Course
-> Module
-> Lesson
-> Video
-> Test
-> Certificate

The learner may:

- browse courses;
- open modules and lessons;
- watch public or restricted video content;
- complete tests or assessments;
- track progress through the learning path;
- receive a certificate when appropriate.

Access can vary by role and content type. Some materials may be public, while others are available only to registered users, instructors, or administrators. The system should support role-based access without making the content model unrealistic or overly generic.

---

## 9. Equipment Flow

A user follows a general buying and discovery flow:

Browse catalog
-> Select equipment
-> Check compatibility with rules
-> Contact seller / create order
-> Track order

The platform may allow users to explore products and compare equipment options. It may also connect equipment items to rule requirements, to help users understand whether an item is compatible with a regulation or competition standard.

The platform should not assume a full e-commerce implementation in the first version. The domain may support browsing, compatibility checks, and order initiation without requiring a full marketplace system.

---

## 10. Media Flow

Users interact with media in several contexts:

- training videos;
- tournament videos;
- photos;
- documents;
- regulations and educational materials.

Media access depends on the audience:

- public: general public-facing content;
- registered user: community access;
- instructor: professional learning materials;
- judge: competition and judging documentation;
- administrator: moderation and management access.

The user journey is not just download or streaming. It includes discovery, classification, access control, and context-aware usage across the platform.

---

## 11. Administrator Flow

Administrators manage the platform's core operations. Their responsibilities include:

- managing users and profiles;
- assigning or adjusting roles;
- managing content and educational materials;
- managing rules and archived regulation versions;
- moderating equipment listings and product information;
- managing tournaments and event records;
- maintaining visibility and accuracy of platform content.

The administrator is a governance role, not a replacement for community or domain-specific roles.

---

## 12. MVP User Scenarios

The minimum viable first version should focus on the most important user journeys:

1. Visitor views content.
2. User registration.
3. Courses and lessons.
4. Rules library.
5. Tournament pages.
6. Athlete profiles.
7. Basic equipment catalog.

This first version keeps the product focused on discovery, education, rules, and basic community participation before broadening into advanced tournament management or marketplace functionality.

---

## 13. Future Scenarios

These scenarios may be relevant later, but they are not part of the initial product scope:

- judge accreditation;
- certificates and formal recognition;
- ratings and standings;
- tournament registration workflows;
- advanced tournament management;
- seller accounts;
- marketplace operations.

These scenarios may be introduced later when the official requirements and platform maturity justify them.

---

## Summary

The main user journeys show that the platform is built around education, rules, tournament participation, community identity, and structured access. The business value is not only in competition management, but in creating an integrated ecosystem where users can learn, compete, judge, organize, and participate according to official regulations and historical records.
